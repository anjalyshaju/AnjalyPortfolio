Behind the project / End-to-end design process

From a music experience brief to a usable interface.

A design-process narrative based on the supplied Music Player screens. It explains how a designer can move from the product requirements to an organized, responsive listening experience.

01

Understand the brief

Start with the visible product requirements: music discovery, streaming, navigation, and a consistent experience across desktop and mobile. Identify the primary journeys rather than jumping directly into colors.

DESIGN OUTPUTRequirements list · Product goals
02

Identify the user problem

Frame the core design challenge: people should find music and control playback without losing context. Prioritize discoverability, familiar controls and clear visual hierarchy.

DESIGN OUTPUTProblem statement · UX priorities
03

Map the listening journeys

Organize the path from first launch and sign-in to browsing recommendations, choosing a track, and controlling playback. Sketch how these actions connect across screen sizes.

DESIGN OUTPUTTask flows · Navigation structure
04

Wireframe key screens

Block out the desktop music library and the mobile splash, sign-in, home, playlist, and player views. Check that navigation and playback controls remain easy to reach.

DESIGN OUTPUTLow-fidelity layouts · Screen structure
05

Build the visual system

Explore the immersive purple palette, readable typography, music artwork, reusable controls, and consistent spacing visible in the final concept.

DESIGN OUTPUTColor palette · Type · Components
06

Prototype and refine

Connect the screens into a realistic listening flow. Review discoverability, control clarity, text legibility, and responsive behavior; revise where the journey feels difficult.

DESIGN OUTPUTClickable prototype · Review checklist
07

Prepare the final presentation

Present desktop, tablet and mobile mockups, the final screen set, and the component decisions that tie the experience together.

DESIGN OUTPUTFinal UI screens · Responsive showcase

Process narrative based on the available project visuals and case-study information. Where research, testing, or deliverables are not documented, steps describe the intended design approach rather than verified completed activities.
